## What is Adobe Commerce Storefront?

Adobe Commerce Storefront lets you build a high-performance online store on Adobe Commerce — fast enough to score near-perfect on web performance benchmarks, and flexible enough to match any brand's design and shopping experience.

Pages are served by <Term>Edge Delivery Services</Term> (EDS), Adobe's global delivery network. EDS turns merchant-authored documents into HTML at the edge, close to shoppers around the world. Merchants edit content the way they edit a document. You write code. Neither gets in the other's way.

Your job as a developer is connecting Commerce to the page. You start from the Commerce boilerplate — a Git repository with a working storefront already inside it. Then you add <Term term="Drop-in components">drop-in components</Term> for each Commerce experience you need: cart, checkout, product details, search, and accounts. Drop-ins call Adobe Commerce <Term>GraphQL</Term> APIs directly from the shopper's browser, so data is always live without a server rendering UI in between. You customize how they look and behave. You don't build Commerce UI from scratch.

Many developers have a working storefront preview in about a day.

<Aside type="note" title="About drop-in licensing">
Drop-in components ship under an Adobe Commerce as a Cloud Service or Adobe Commerce Optimizer license. Commerce PaaS alone does not include drop-in licensing unless your organization adds ACO. Read [Backend options](/get-started/backends/) for how backend types map to prerequisites, and [Licensing requirements](/licensing/) for eligibility and access.
</Aside>

## New here? Start here.

You'll follow the numbered steps below in order. Each step prepares you for the next. The path starts with tools and a short picture of how pages, your repository, and drop-in components fit together. Then you'll create a preview, run the boilerplate on your computer, learn how drop-ins work in code, connect the storefront to Commerce, and finish with the launch checklist. Open the first link when you are ready. You can bookmark this page if you want an easy return point from the sidebar.

<Callouts columnCount={1}>
1. [Before you start](/get-started/before-you-start/) — Gather your tools, accounts, and Commerce access before you open Site Creator. About 20 minutes.
1. [Storefront Architecture](/get-started/architecture/) — Five short topics: how pages load, what blocks and drop-ins are, how drop-ins coordinate, and how Commerce services connect. Build your mental model before you build code. About 25 minutes.
1. [Backend options](/get-started/backends/) — Identify your Commerce backend (Commerce PaaS, Adobe Commerce as a Cloud Service, or Adobe Commerce Optimizer) and confirm what you need before you create a site.
1. [Create a storefront](/get-started/create-storefront/) — Provision your repo and preview URL with Site Creator in DA.live. This is where your first working preview comes from.
1. [Boilerplate getting started](/boilerplate/getting-started/) — Run the Commerce boilerplate on your machine. You'll have a locally running storefront with Commerce blocks already connected.
1. [Boilerplate configuration](/boilerplate/configuration/) — `config.json`, headers, and paths that match your production environment.
1. [Introduction to drop-ins](/dropins/all/introduction/) and [Using drop-ins](/dropins/all/quick-start/) — What drop-in components are, the initializer import pattern, slots, styling, and labels. Read these before you customize any Commerce UI.
1. [Storefront configuration](/setup/configuration/commerce-configuration/) — Connect the storefront to Commerce endpoints and services.
1. [Launch checklist](/setup/launch/) — Steps to finish before you treat the site as production-ready.
</Callouts>
